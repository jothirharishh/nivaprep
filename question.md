1. Synonyms

How they ask

Choose the appropriate synonym for the italicised word.

Example: The word rapid means:

(a) Slow  (b) Fast  (c) Weak  (d) Late

Prepare: Textbook vocabulary, word meanings and contextual meanings.




2. Antonyms

How they ask

Choose the opposite meaning of the underlined word.

Example: The antonym of generous is:

(a) Kind  (b) Helpful  (c) Selfish  (d) Honest

Prepare: Common opposites and words used in prose passages.




3. Word formation

How they ask

Choose the correct plural, prefix, suffix or compound word.

Examples:
- Choose the correct plural form of loaf.
- Add the appropriate prefix to accurate.
- Pair a word with house to make a compound word.
Prepare: Plurals, prefixes, suffixes, compound words and derivatives.




4. Fill in the blank using grammar

How they ask

Complete a sentence with the most appropriate option.

The tested grammar can include:
- Phrasal verbs
- Prepositions
- Verb tenses
- Linkers and conjunctions
Example: My mother called me _____ I was playing football.

(a) while  (b) or  (c) by  (d) if

Prepare: Rules plus sentence-based practice, not just definitions.




5. Abbreviations and expansions

How they ask

Choose the correct expansion of an abbreviation or identify its meaning.

Example: Expand the abbreviation BCE.

Prepare: Abbreviations and their full forms found in the prescribed material.



English takeaway: Practise each grammar topic in two ways: identify the correct rule and apply it to a sentence. These are the main styles demonstrated by the inspected paper.

The four ways to practise Mathematics MCQs
Type A

Recall

Know definitions, properties, standard results and formulas.

Type B

Direct calculation

Substitute the given values into a formula and evaluate the answer.

Type C

Application

Translate a short real-life situation into an equation or diagram.

Type D

Reverse reasoning

Given a result or condition, work backwards to find the unknown value.


Mathematics takeaway: For every chapter, practise at least a few formula-based, conceptual and application MCQs. Memorising formulas alone will not prepare you for questions that reverse the condition or present it as a word problem.

The most useful Science preparation method
For every important textbook concept, prepare these four details:
- Definition: What does the term mean?
- Property: What is its defining characteristic?
- Reason: Why does it happen?
- Application: What happens when a value, condition or variable changes?
For example, when studying a physics formula, do not stop at memorising it. Practise questions asking how the answer changes when one input doubles, halves or becomes zero.

Science takeaway: The board can test the same concept through a direct fact, a short explanation, a formula-based calculation or a situation-based MCQ.
